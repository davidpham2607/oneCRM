## Give your team the context behind the work

The person who wins an engagement may not be the person delivering it. Keep the accepted quote, client contacts, and notes on their goals together so the delivery lead can prepare without asking the client to repeat the brief.

- The accepted quote as a reference for the agreed work

- Client contacts and background notes for the delivery lead

- Project files that stay with the engagement

## Make the next step clear when several people share the work

An engagement can pass from research to review to a final report. Create phases for those steps, assign the tasks, and set due dates so the engagement lead can check progress without collecting separate updates.

- Phases that reflect how your firm delivers an engagement

- An assignee and deadline for each piece of work

- Task status visible to the people coordinating delivery

## Prepare the client bill without chasing the whole team

When several people contribute to an engagement, the billable work is easy to scatter. Have the team record time and expenses against the project, giving the person preparing the invoice one place to find the hours and costs.

- Team time entries attached to the engagement

- Receipts and billable expenses alongside the work

- Project invoicing from the records your team has kept
